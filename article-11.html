Digital Twins for a Changing World

Technology briefing • 5 min read

A digital twin connects a digital model of a real-world system to data about how that system behaves.

Unlike a static 3D model, a digital twin can be updated with information from sensors, maintenance records, or operational systems. Depending on its purpose, it may represent a component, a factory line, a building, or a larger infrastructure network.

From visibility to simulation

A well-designed twin can help teams understand current conditions, compare scenarios, and spot patterns that are difficult to see across separate data sources. Engineers may use it to explore design changes or plan maintenance before making costly real-world adjustments.

Data quality sets the limits

A twin is only as useful as its underlying data and assumptions. Missing sensors, delayed updates, or inaccurate models can create a misleading picture. Teams should make uncertainty visible and validate predictions against observed outcomes.

Interoperability and security

Real systems often rely on software from multiple vendors, so connecting data can be a major challenge. Because twins may expose sensitive operational details, access control, secure data flows, and clear ownership are essential parts of deployment.

Start with a specific decision

Digital twins are not valuable simply because they are visually impressive. A focused project begins with a concrete question—such as how to reduce downtime or energy use—and measures whether the model helps people make a better decision.
