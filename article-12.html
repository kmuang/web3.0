Preparing for Post-Quantum Security

Technology briefing • 6 min read

Organizations can prepare for future cryptographic changes before large-scale quantum computers become practical.

Many widely used public-key cryptographic systems rely on mathematical problems that are difficult for today's computers. A sufficiently capable quantum computer could weaken some of those systems. No one can predict exactly when such machines will be available, but upgrading cryptography across complex technology estates takes time.

Understand where cryptography is used

The first step is an inventory: identify which products, protocols, certificates, devices, and vendors rely on vulnerable algorithms. Cryptography can be hidden inside dependencies and long-lived equipment, making visibility a practical challenge.

Plan for crypto agility

Crypto agility is the ability to replace cryptographic components without redesigning an entire system. Clear ownership, modular implementations, tested update paths, and vendor coordination can make future transitions safer and less disruptive.

Protect data with a long lifetime

Sensitive information that must remain confidential for many years deserves particular attention. An adversary could collect encrypted information today in the hope of decrypting it later if the protection becomes weaker. Risk assessments should consider how long data needs to stay private.

Transition carefully

New algorithms and standards need testing for compatibility, performance, and implementation security. Avoid improvised cryptographic designs; follow trusted standards and expert guidance, and keep migration decisions documented and reviewable.

Preparation is broader than a single upgrade

Post-quantum readiness is an ongoing technology and governance effort, not a switch to flip once. Regular inventories, procurement requirements, and upgrade plans help organizations adapt as standards and products mature.
