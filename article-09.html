Digital Identity Beyond Passwords

Technology briefing • 5 min read

New identity tools aim to make sign-in safer and give people more control over what they share.

Passwords remain a common weak point: they can be reused, phished, or exposed in data breaches. Passkeys use public-key cryptography and device-based verification to make many sign-ins resistant to common phishing techniques, while reducing reliance on memorized secrets.

Credentials you can present

Verifiable credentials are digitally signed claims issued by an organization and held by a person or their device. In principle, someone could prove a specific attribute—such as eligibility—without sharing an entire document. The issuer, format, and verification process still matter.

Decentralized does not mean risk-free

Some identity systems use wallets and cryptographic proofs instead of a single central account provider. This can reduce certain dependencies, but it also shifts responsibility: people need secure recovery options, and services need accessible ways to verify credentials.

Privacy and inclusion by design

Identity systems should disclose as little data as possible, make consent understandable, and avoid tracking people across unrelated services. They also need non-digital alternatives and support for people who lose devices or cannot use a particular technology.

A gradual transition

Passkeys and verifiable credentials solve different problems, and neither removes the need for good account recovery or responsible data handling. The strongest systems combine secure authentication with clear privacy choices and practical support when things go wrong.
